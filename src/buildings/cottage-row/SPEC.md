# Cottage terrace

Byelaw brick terrace of two-up two-down houses (c. 1890), front +Z, `count` houses at 4.8 m centres (default 3), single part `main` (pipeline: raw, places and finishes itself). Used by the Clearance Zone ('terrace', 'cottages'), the Railway Quarter and a contract.

Construction: 230 mm solid brick (Flemish bond units, lime plaster on room faces), stone sills/lintels, two-over-two sashes and panelled doors as dormant joinery detail (`_shared/vernacular.ts`); timber ground floor; first floor 0.22 m joisted, pocketed into the party walls; slate roof ~38° per house on party-wall gables; stacks on flue walls; single-storey scullery outriggers paired on a shared wall with lean-to slate roofs.

Load paths: roof slabs seat on the front/back wall tops and lean on the party-wall gable triangles; first floor bears on the lower party-wall lift and carries the upper lift; outrigger roofs seat on the yard walls and abut the shared wall and the back wall; each stack rises from its flue wall in a neck of the wall's width and stands 30 mm clear of the slopes and gables (flashing gap), so it bears on its wall alone; each ground-floor chimney breast is bonded into its flue wall's ground lift (one compound body).

Floors are `timberDeck` (150×22 boards on 50 mm joists at 400 centres, lath-and-plaster ceiling as cosmetic skins that powder rather than fly as cards). Group 'terrace' is red brick with tall 4-pot stacks; any other group ('cottages') has its fronts rendered and painted a different pastel per house over a dark painted plinth (render units laid white and tinted per house), buff-tinted side and back brick, short pots, a lighter roof and its own door palette. Weathering: base dirt, random dark bricks, sill streaks; stacks soot up toward a black oversailing course with soot run-off streaks below it, a dark cement flaunching swept up to the pots, and pots blackened at the rim and grimy at the bed (`_shared/roofs.ts` stackShaft, shared with the semis, shop and pub).

Known gaps (package): no stairwell, stair or furniture (the stair and spine wall were dropped for the Clearance piece budget in round 4); brick colour is a tint over one brick texture (buff reads orange-red, hence the rendered 'cottages' fronts); the render is painted-plaster cells about 0.6 m, jittered row by row, each house's paint stopping at the party line over a dark painted plinth, the reveals of doors and windows rendered too (the soffits over them show the lintel); pots are plain octagonal clay with a moulded rim, sooted at the top, bedded in a swept cement flaunching; slates and ridge tiles close up to the ridge and eaves (a course cut by the slab's edge is shortened, not narrowed); front/back walls are one member per storey between openings; the gable shows a straight joint where the gable member meets the corner member (critic N8: units cannot cross members); roof slabs ≤1.2 m still land as intact slabs; with a flue wall's ground lift deleted, the roof over it sags in over 2-40 s because its slabs seat on the untouched front and back walls (floors 0.4 s, stack 1.0 s, upper lift 1.2 s).
Known gaps (engine, not package-fixable; critic loop 1 round 4): damaged ground lifts carve to load-bearing stubs instead of vanishing; roof hangs over the void after a blast; brick clumps fly as see-through lattices with no mortar or fines, and land as clean coursed rafts; slabs don't fracture on impact (roofs land or stand on edge whole); explosive smoke is black, long-lived and sprite-edged and burning timber draws as emissive bars; fires after the deletion test keep the site smoky past 90 s; 600-1900 bodies still awake 60 s after an event (std blast 1885, flue blast 603, deletion 1890). Map-owned: grid meter heads (M2), 3-house rows (M4); the forecourts (M3) now have their front walls, gates, paths and dividers in the Clearance map.
Known gaps (engine, carried from critic loop 1 round 4 findings): timber floor and ceiling decks float or cantilever off a party wall 65-86 s after every event (N11; frozen or viewer-dependent collapse); in the game the four-pot flue stack still stands at 8 s after its wall's deletion while headless has it down by 2 s; a flat skin unit (render, plaster) draws its texture scaled to the cell, so a render cell repeats the same texture patch (the painted render reads as faint repeated blotches, no longer a tinted patchwork); cylinder units draw a chamfer at each of their 16 facets that edge-wear lightens into stripes, so pots are octagonal.
